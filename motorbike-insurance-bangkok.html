Motorbike Rental Insurance in Bangkok: What's Covered If You Crash or It's Stolen

"What happens if something goes wrong?" is the second most common question we get, right after licenses. Here's a plain-English breakdown of deposits, damage, theft, and who pays for what.

What's included with a Roadmates rental

Every bike we rent comes with basic third-party liability coverage (required by Thai law for registered vehicles) and a helmet. We take a cash or card-hold deposit at pickup, refunded in full when the bike comes back in the condition it left in. We explain the deposit amount and what it covers before you sign anything — no surprise deductions later.

What insurance actually covers

Third-party liability covers injury or damage you cause to another person or their vehicle in an accident — it does not cover damage to the rental bike itself, and it does not cover your own medical costs. If you want those covered, arrange separate travel or personal accident insurance before your trip; most travel policies include this, but check the fine print for "motorbike" or "scooter" exclusions, which are common.

What you're liable for (deposit and excess)

Cosmetic scratches from normal use aren't charged. Damage from an accident, a drop, or a stolen/missing part is deducted from your deposit up to the actual repair cost — we always show you the repair invoice, we don't estimate or guess. If repair costs exceed the deposit (rare, usually only in a serious crash), we discuss it directly with you rather than silently charging a card.

What happens if the bike is stolen

Report it to the nearest police station immediately and get a police report (สคบ.), then contact us right away — don't wait. A police report is required for any insurance claim and for us to close out the rental correctly. This is also exactly why we ask you to always park where other bikes are parked and, ideally, use a U-lock or disc lock we provide, especially overnight.

What happens in an accident with another vehicle

Don't move the vehicles until police arrive if anyone is injured or there's a dispute over fault (standard practice in Thailand). Take photos, get the other party's details, and call us — we can usually talk you through the process in English over the phone. Minor scrapes with no injury and mutual agreement are often settled on the spot; anything more serious goes through police and insurance.

Tips to avoid problems altogether

Wear the helmet we give you, every time — it's the single biggest factor in accident severity and the rule police check most. Don't ride after drinking. Check the fuel and existing scratches together with us at pickup so there's no ambiguity later. And save our WhatsApp number before you ride off — most issues are solved with a five-minute message, not a drama.
