Where to Park a Motorbike Safely in Bangkok

Bangkok has plenty of free scooter parking — you just need to know where to look. Here's the neighborhood-by-neighborhood version we give every renter at pickup.

The golden rule: park where other bikes already are

This is the single most reliable trick in the city. If you see a row of motorbikes parked outside a shophouse, 7-Eleven, or restaurant, that spot is safe and accepted — locals wouldn't leave their own bikes there otherwise. It also means you're parking somewhere with informal "eyes on the street," which matters more for security than any lock.

Sathorn & Silom

Our home turf. Most office buildings and condos along Sathorn and Silom have dedicated motorbike parking around the side or rear — look for a security guard's booth, which usually means a monitored lot. Soi 19 and the surrounding sois have plenty of street-level spots outside restaurants and 7-Elevens; avoid blocking building entrances or fire exits, which guards will move you on from quickly.

Sukhumvit

Sukhumvit's side sois (soi 11, 23, 33, 55/Thonglor) are dense with bars, restaurants, and malls that have their own motorbike parking zones, often marked with painted lines and sometimes a small fee (10-20 baht) collected by an attendant — pay it, it's effectively parking insurance. Avoid the main Sukhumvit sidewalk itself; it's regularly cleared by BTS-area enforcement.

Old City / Rattanakosin

Around the Grand Palace, Wat Pho, and Khao San, parking is tighter and more heavily policed due to tourist foot traffic. Look for the paid motorbike lots that pop up near major temples (20-30 baht, attendant-run) rather than squeezing onto a sidewalk — illegally parked bikes here get ticketed or towed more often than anywhere else in the city.

Malls and office buildings

Nearly every mall (Silom Complex, Central, Terminal 21, EmQuartier) has a motorbike section in the basement or a side entrance of the car park, usually free or a few baht, and always guarded — genuinely one of the safest overnight options if you're leaving a bike for hours. Ask a security guard "จอดมอไซค์ตรงไหนครับ/คะ" (jòt maw-sai dtrong nǎi) and they'll point you there even without English.

What not to do

Don't block a sidewalk ramp, fire exit, or driveway — you'll come back to a fine, a moved bike, or worse. Don't leave valuables in the seat compartment even when locked. Use the disc lock we provide whenever you're parking for more than an hour, especially overnight, and always favor a lit, populated spot over an empty side street even if it's a few minutes further to walk.
